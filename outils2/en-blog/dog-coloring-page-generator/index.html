Which settings to use — a quick guide by fur type

Dogs come in all shapes and coat types, and the same settings won't work equally well for every breed. Here's how to dial things in depending on your dog's coat:

Fur type Detail level Smoothing Line thickness
Short-haired (Labrador, Beagle, Boxer) Medium (40–60) Low to medium Medium — clean outlines come naturally
Long-haired smooth (Golden, Border Collie) Medium (40–55) Medium Medium — the fur flow adds natural detail
Fluffy / double coat (Husky, Samoyed) Low to medium (30–45) Low — keep the texture Thin — lets the fur texture breathe
Curly / wavy (Poodle, Doodles) High (60–75) High — simplify the curls Thick — helps curls stay readable
Wiry (Schnauzer, Terriers) Medium-high (50–65) Medium Medium — captures the textured look well
📸 Photo tip — the one thing that matters most

The single biggest factor in getting a clean coloring page isn't the settings — it's the photo. A sharp, well-lit portrait with your dog's face filling most of the frame will always beat a great photo of a dog far in the distance. If possible, take the photo outdoors in soft natural light with a plain fence, wall, or lawn in the background.

🖨 Print tip — paper makes the difference

Standard 80gsm printer paper works fine for a quick test, but if you're coloring with markers or want a gift-worthy result, use 120–160gsm card stock. It holds color better, doesn't warp when wet, and feels more like a proper coloring page. For the best possible quality, matte photo paper at 200gsm gives a beautiful surface for pencil work.
