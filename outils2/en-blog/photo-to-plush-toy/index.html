Tips for the most realistic plush effect

  • Use a portrait with visible facial features. The algorithm relies on detecting eyes, nose, and mouth to place stitches correctly.
  • Avoid strong shadows. Even lighting prevents the wool texture from looking muddy.
  • Try a neutral background. While the tool can remove backgrounds, a clean backdrop yields a sharper result.
  • Experiment with eye styles. Button eyes give a classic toy feel, while embroidered eyes look sweeter.
  • Don’t oversaturate the texture. Sometimes a light wool pass looks more natural than maximum thickness.

How does the tool actually work? (the nerdy part)

When you upload a photo, the Plush Customizer runs a local JavaScript pipeline that performs several steps:

  1. Face detection: It identifies the main subject and separates it from the background.
  2. Edge mapping: Contours of eyes, nose, and mouth are traced to place stitches precisely.
  3. Texture synthesis: A procedural wool pattern (like a cross‑hatch knit) is overlaid and distorted to follow facial curves.
  4. Color averaging: The dominant colors of your photo are extracted and mapped to a soft, fabric‑like palette.
  5. Lighting and shadows: A digital drop shadow and subtle highlights create the 3D plush illusion.

Because all of this happens inside your browser, there’s zero server cost — and zero privacy risk. You can even disconnect the internet after loading the page and it still works.
